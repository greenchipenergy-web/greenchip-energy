PM Surya Ghar ₹78,000 Subsidy: How Hyderabad Homes Claim It

The PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana is the central government's flagship residential rooftop solar subsidy scheme. Under its current structure, eligible homeowners receive up to ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW — totalling ₹78,000 for a 3 kW system via Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) to the applicant's bank account. Systems above 3 kW still qualify, but the central subsidy is capped at ₹78,000. Telangana's Utility-Led Aggregation (ULA) programme has further expanded the pipeline, targeting 20.33 lakh households with 4,066 MW of rooftop capacity [The New Indian Express, October 2026].

To claim the subsidy in Hyderabad, follow these steps: (1) Register on the National Portal (pmsuryaghar.gov.in) with your electricity consumer number; (2) Select a DISCOM-empanelled installer from the portal's approved vendor list; (3) The installer submits the system design and DISCOM application on your behalf; (4) After TSSPDCL approves the net-metering proposal and the system is commissioned, the subsidy amount is disbursed via DBT. Typical processing takes 30–60 days post-commissioning, though individual timelines vary. Working with a proactive solar installation company in Hyderabad that manages the portal registration, documentation, and follow-ups can prevent the most common rejection reasons — mismatched consumer numbers, incomplete structural certificates, and non-ALMM panel selections.

TSSPDCL Net-Metering Process & Approval Timeline

On 15 November 2025, the Telangana Electricity Regulatory Commission notified the Rooftop Solar PV Grid Interactive Systems Regulation 2025 (No. 1 of 2025), permitting net metering up to 500 kWp for residential consumers and capping rooftop capacity at 100% of the sanctioned load [TGSPDCL Guidelines]. This regulation significantly expanded the legal framework for residential solar in Hyderabad, removing earlier capacity restrictions and enabling homeowners to install systems sized to their full sanctioned load.

The net-metering application process works as follows: (1) Your installer submits an application to the local TSSPDCL subdivision office with the system design, single-line diagram, and equipment specifications; (2) A TSSPDCL engineer conducts a site inspection; (3) Upon approval, the installer commissions the system with a bidirectional (net) meter provided by the DISCOM; (4) The meter is sealed and the plant enters commercial operation. Typical approval timelines in Hyderabad range from 15–45 working days, depending on local office workload and documentation completeness. The 2025 regulation also permits virtual net metering and group net metering provisions, which open possibilities for apartment complexes and gated communities — a growing segment in areas like Gachibowli and Hitech City where independent-roof access is limited.
