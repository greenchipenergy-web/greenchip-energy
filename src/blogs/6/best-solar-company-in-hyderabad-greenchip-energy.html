PM Surya Ghar ₹78,000 Subsidy: How Hyderabad Homes Claim It

The PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana, launched 13 February 2024, is the single largest driver of residential solar adoption in Hyderabad. Under the scheme, homeowners receive a central subsidy of ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 per kW for the third kW, capped at ₹78,000 for systems of 3 kW and above, as confirmed by the Press Information Bureau. Telangana additionally offers a state top-up of ₹20,000 to ₹60,000 through TSREDCO, depending on system size. Together, these stacked subsidies can offset nearly half the unsubsidised cost of a standard 3 kW installation.

To claim the subsidy, your installer must be registered on the PM Surya Ghar National Portal (pmsuryaghar.gov.in). The process follows five steps: (1) your installer registers the application on the portal, (2) TSSPDCL verifies and grants technical approval, (3) the physical installation is completed, (4) the DISCOM installs a bi-directional net meter and issues commissioning clearance, and (5) the subsidy is disbursed — typically as a direct credit to the installer, reducing your invoice. A structured EPC provider manages this paperwork chain end-to-end; you can contact GreenChip Energy for a solar EPC consultation or any MNRE-empanelled installer to initiate the process.

Keep in mind that subsidy disbursement timelines vary case-to-case based on DISCOM processing speed. Always confirm your installer's empanelment directly on the national portal before signing a contract.

TSSPDCL Net-Metering Process & Approval Timeline

Net metering is the financial engine behind rooftop solar in Hyderabad. Your system feeds surplus daytime electricity into the TSSPDCL grid, your bi-directional meter credits every exported unit, and your monthly bill reflects only net consumption. For a well-sized system, that bill can drop to near zero.

The Telangana Electricity Regulatory Commission (TGERC) overhauled its framework in November 2025 with Regulation No. 1 of 2025, replacing the 2016 rules. Key changes include raising the net-metering cap from 100 kWp to 500 kWp and introducing Group Net Metering and Virtual Net Metering provisions — particularly valuable for apartment complexes and RWAs sharing a common solar installation, per the TGERC gazette notification.

For a typical residential homeowner, the process unfolds in five stages: (1) your installer submits the net-metering application to TSSPDCL with system specifications and single-line diagrams, (2) TSSPDCL issues feasibility and technical approval, (3) the installer completes the physical installation, (4) TSSPDCL inspects the site and installs a bi-directional net meter, and (5) commissioning clearance is granted. Processing timelines typically range from 4 to 8 weeks when documentation is complete, though delays of 2–3 months can occur due to local sub-station load constraints or paperwork errors. Engaging an EPC provider experienced with TSSPDCL's local offices usually shortens this cycle significantly by ensuring first-time-right documentation.

Red Flags to Avoid When Hiring a Hyderabad Solar Company

The rapid expansion of Hyderabad's rooftop solar market has attracted brokers, subcontractors, and unqualified operators offering lower prices but cutting corners on quality, compliance, and warranty. Knowing the red flags before you sign a contract can save you from costly mistakes.

The first major warning sign is demanding 100% payment upfront. Reputable EPC companies structure payments in stages — 10–20% advance, a portion on material delivery, and the balance after commissioning and net-metering approval. Full upfront payment eliminates your leverage entirely. Second, watch for non-ALMM panels. Panels outside MNRE's Approved Models and Manufacturers list may disqualify your subsidy claim and carry unverifiable long-term performance. Always request ALMM registration numbers and cross-check on the official MNRE portal.

Additional red flags include: refusing to provide a GST invoice (which protects warranty rights and subsidy eligibility), quoting without a prior site survey (roof angle, shadow patterns, and structural capacity all affect system design), and vague or missing warranty documentation for both panels and inverters. Some installers also exclude TSSPDCL net-metering handling from the written contract — if it is not in the agreement, assume it will not happen. A structured EPC provider with staged payment milestones, ALMM-compliant components, and end-to-end documentation from engineering design through commissioning eliminates these risks by design.
