PM Surya Ghar ₹78,000 Subsidy: How Hyderabad Homes Claim It

The PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana, launched on February 13, 2024, remains India's most impactful residential solar subsidy programme. According to the Press Information Bureau, by December 2025 over 23.9 lakh households had installed rooftop solar under the scheme, collectively adding 7 GW of clean energy capacity with ₹13,464.6 crore in subsidies already released. For a typical Hyderabad household, the central subsidy covers 40% of benchmark costs for systems up to 3 kW, and 20% for the 3–10 kW range, with the maximum subsidy capped at ₹78,000 per household.

The application process works as follows: register on the national portal (pmsuryaghar.gov.in), select your DISCOM (TSSPDCL or TSNPDCL for Hyderabad), choose an MNRE-empanelled or DISCOM-listed installer, submit your site survey details, receive technical feasibility approval, get the system installed, and then apply for net metering. Once the DISCOM installs the bidirectional meter and generates the commissioning certificate, the subsidy amount is typically credited directly to your bank account. A critical tip: verify that your chosen installer is listed on the national portal's empanelled roster, and ensure every panel and inverter used carries ALMM (Approved List of Models and Manufacturers) certification — non-ALMM components are the single most common reason for subsidy rejection. Telangana had installed 472.9 MW of rooftop solar capacity as of January 2025, representing 14% of the southern region's total, so the state's subsidy disbursement machinery is well-tested.

TSSPDCL Net-Metering Process and Approval Timeline

On August 14, 2025, the Telangana Electricity Regulatory Commission released the Rooftop Solar PV Grid Interactive Systems Regulation 2025, replacing the 2016 framework. The new regulation allows net metering for systems up to 500 kW, group and virtual net metering for systems below 100 kW, and gross metering up to 1 MW. For residential and government consumers, solar capacity can reach up to 100% of sanctioned load; commercial and industrial consumers are capped at 80% of contracted demand. A key grid-safety provision: cumulative rooftop solar capacity on any single distribution transformer cannot exceed 50% of its rated capacity.

The practical net-metering workflow with TSSPDCL is as follows: (1) Submit your application through the DISCOM portal or the PM Surya Ghar national portal with your sanctioned load document, proposed system size, and installer details. (2) TSSPDCL conducts a technical feasibility check — typically 7–15 working days — to confirm your distribution transformer has headroom. (3) Once approved, your installer completes the installation and submits the commissioning report. (4) TSSPDCL installs the bidirectional (net) meter and activates the arrangement. The application fee is ₹2,500 for LT (low-tension) connections and ₹15,000 for HT (high-tension) connections. A competent solar company handles this entire workflow on your behalf, but always confirm this in writing before signing the contract — delays at the DISCOM stage are the most common source of project overrun in Hyderabad.

Contract & Warranty

DISCOM & Subsidy Handling

  1. Does the company handle TSSPDCL/TSNPDCL net-metering paperwork in-house?
  2. Do they assist with the PM Surya Ghar subsidy application on the national portal?
  3. Is there a written timeline commitment for installation and grid synchronization?

Red Flags to Avoid When Hiring a Hyderabad Solar Company

The Hyderabad solar market's rapid growth has attracted both excellent installers and opportunistic operators. The most damaging red flag is demanding 100% payment upfront before any site survey or material procurement. Reputable companies typically collect 10–30% as an advance, with the balance due after successful installation and DISCOM commissioning. Another common trap is non-ALMM panels — some contractors quote lower prices using panels that are not on the MNRE-approved list, which disqualifies your PM Surya Ghar subsidy claim entirely and may cause TSSPDCL to reject your net-metering application.

Additional warning signs include: refusing to provide a GST invoice (critical for subsidy and warranty claims), vague or verbal-only warranty promises, no written scope of work detailing panel brand/model and inverter specifications, and quoting prices that are dramatically below the market range (which usually signals component substitution or corner-cutting on mounting structures and cabling). Also watch out for companies that promise a specific electricity generation number without conducting a proper shadow analysis and roof-orientation survey — generation estimates should always come with a disclaimer referencing actual site conditions. For commercial and industrial buyers considering PPA/OPEX models, ensure the contract clearly states tariff escalation clauses, lock-in periods, and handover terms at end-of-contract. A thorough, transparent proposal is the single best indicator of a professional solar company.

Yes. Telangana's 2025 regulation introduced group and virtual net metering for systems below 100 kW, allowing apartment residents and gated communities to share a common rooftop solar installation and receive individual billing credits — even if their flats don't have direct roof access.

Can I get solar installed without paying upfront?

What documents do I need for TSSPDCL net metering?

You need your latest electricity bill, sanctioned load document, Aadhaar, property ownership proof, proposed system details from your installer, and the application fee (₹2,500 for LT connections). Your solar company should handle the full application on your behalf.
