What Is a Solar EPC Company and How Does It Work in India?

Solar EPC stands for Engineering, Procurement, and Construction—a turnkey project delivery model in which a single contractor handles the full lifecycle of a solar power plant. The Engineering phase covers system design, shade analysis, electrical layout, and structural planning. Procurement involves sourcing solar panels, inverters, mounting structures, and balance-of-system components. Construction covers on-site installation, cabling, and grid integration. Finally, commissioning validates performance and hands over a working asset to the buyer.

In India, the EPC model has become the default engagement structure across all three buyer segments—residential homeowners seeking lower bills, commercial and industrial (C&I) facility owners targeting capex-free rooftop or ground-mount systems, and utility-scale developers building multi-megawatt plants. A turnkey provider bundles every phase under one contract, which reduces coordination risk and gives the buyer a single point of accountability. Providers offering smart solar EPC solutions in India exemplify this single-provider approach across residential, C&I, and utility segments. Some contractors also offer Operations & Maintenance (O&M) as an add-on, covering panel cleaning, inverter monitoring, and performance reporting over the plant's 25-year life. The critical distinction for buyers is this: an EPC contractor is not merely an installer. A genuine EPC company provides engineering design capability, procures components directly from manufacturers, and executes construction with an in-house or tightly managed workforce. When you hire a solar EPC contractor, you are hiring a company that owns every stage of delivery—and therefore every stage of risk.

Segment-Wise EPC Profiles: Utility-Scale, C&I & Residential

Mid-size national and regional players with strong DISCOM liaison capability and PPA/OPEX engagement models often deliver the best fit. Providers offering turnkey delivery with capex-free options are especially attractive for C&I clients who want solar savings without upfront capital.

Residential Rooftop (1–10 kW): The fastest-growing segment, driven by PM Surya Ghar subsidies. Homeowners should hire a solar EPC contractor who is registered with the local DISCOM, uses ALMM-listed modules, and provides written warranty terms. Engineering & Design Capability (20%): Does the contractor have in-house electrical and structural engineers? Do they perform shade analysis and system sizing using industry-standard software? A company that outsources design has limited ability to troubleshoot post-installation issues.

PM Surya Ghar Subsidy, Net-Metering & MNRE Empanelment Checklist

The PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana has become India's single largest demand driver for residential rooftop solar. By December 2025, the scheme had covered 26,14,446 households with 20,85,514 rooftop systems installed and ₹14,771.82 crore disbursed as Central Financial Assistance, according to the Press Information Bureau. However, the scheme's simplified vendor registration has attracted temporary players—cable-TV operators, electricians, and interior designers—raising concerns about long-term warranty support once the scheme winds down around March 2027.

Before you hire a solar EPC contractor for a subsidised residential project, verify these non-negotiables:

  • MNRE Empanelment or DISCOM Registration: The contractor should be registered on the PM Surya Ghar national portal and empanelled with your state DISCOM. Empanelment is state-specific—no single contractor is approved for every state.
  • ALMM-Listed Modules: Under MNRE's ALMM Order (2019), only modules from manufacturers and models on the current ALMM list qualify for government-subsidised projects. The list was last updated on 19 September 2025. Using non-listed modules disqualifies the project from subsidy and can void manufacturer warranties.
  • Written Warranty Terms: MNRE-standard agreements mandate a minimum 8-year product warranty on panels and inverters, and a peak-watt capacity warranty of at least 90% at 10 years and 80% at 25 years. Ensure these terms are written into your contract and transferable to you—not held by the vendor.
  • Net-Metering Processing: Confirm the contractor handles DISCOM net-metering application, meter installation, and commissioning inspection as part of their scope.

Reputable EPC providers handle residential rooftop projects end-to-end—from design through subsidy documentation and grid synchronization.

Red Flags & Risk Checklist Before Signing an EPC Contract

Even with a scorecard in hand, certain warning signs should stop you from signing. Watch for these red flags:

No written contract or vague scope of work: A legitimate EPC contractor provides a detailed agreement specifying system capacity, component makes and models, warranty terms, delivery timeline, commissioning date, and payment milestones. If the contractor offers only a handwritten quotation or refuses to commit specifications to paper, walk away.

Unverifiable references: Ask for at least three completed project references with customer contact details. Call one. Visit one if possible. Contractors who cannot produce references for your specific segment lack the track record you need.

Below-market pricing that feels too good: If a quote is 20–30% below the benchmarks listed earlier, the contractor is likely cutting corners on module quality, inverter brand, mounting structure thickness, or labour. Under-designed systems fail within 3–5 years.

No O&M plan or post-installation support: Solar plants need periodic cleaning, inverter firmware updates, and performance monitoring. A contractor who installs and disappears leaves you with a degrading asset. Ask for written O&M terms, response-time commitments, and AMC pricing before signing.

Pressure to skip the site assessment: A proper EPC engagement starts with a physical site visit—roof structural assessment, shadow analysis, electrical audit, and grid connectivity check. Contractors who quote without visiting your site are guessing, not engineering.

No mention of ALMM or BIS compliance: If the proposal does not reference ALMM-listed modules and BIS-registered inverters, the contractor may install grey-market or non-compliant components that disqualify your subsidy claim. These red flags apply equally whether you engage a national player or a regional contractor. Due diligence before signing saves decades of regret.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a solar EPC contractor and how does it work?

A solar EPC (Engineering, Procurement, and Construction) contractor is a turnkey provider that handles system design, component sourcing, on-site installation, and commissioning under a single contract—giving the buyer one point of accountability for the entire project.

How much does it cost to hire a solar EPC contractor in India in 2026?

Indicative EPC costs range from ₹35–50/W for residential rooftop to ₹22–30/W for utility-scale projects, depending on scale, module technology, and site conditions. Always request a site-assessed quotation.

How do I verify if a solar EPC contractor is MNRE-empanelled?

Check MNRE's official channel-partner list or verify the contractor's registration on the PM Surya Ghar national portal. Empanelment is state-specific and category-specific.

Can I claim PM Surya Ghar subsidy if my EPC contractor uses non-ALMM modules?

No. Under MNRE's ALMM Order, only modules from the current ALMM list qualify for government subsidies, net-metering, and open-access projects. Using non-ALMM modules disqualifies the subsidy claim.

What warranty should a solar EPC contractor provide?

MNRE-standard agreements require a minimum 8-year product warranty on panels and inverters, and a capacity warranty of at least 90% at 10 years and 80% at 25 years. Ensure warranty terms are written into your contract.

Should I choose a capex or PPA model when hiring a solar EPC contractor?

Capex means you own the system and claim all savings after payback. PPA/OPEX means the contractor owns the system and you pay per unit of electricity generated. Your choice depends on capital availability and risk appetite.

What are the biggest red flags when evaluating a solar EPC contractor?

Key red flags include: no written contract, unverifiable references, below-market pricing, no O&M plan, pressure to skip the site assessment, and no mention of ALMM or BIS compliance in the proposal.
